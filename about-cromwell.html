About Cromwell, Connecticut

Cromwell is a small town on the Connecticut River with a long memory and a straightforward government.

Here's the short version of where it came from and how it works.

History

From “Upper Houses” to its own town

The first settlers arrived in Cromwell in 1651. At the time the area wasn't a town at all — it was known as the Upper Houses section of Middletown.

It stayed that way for two centuries. Then, in 1851, Cromwell became a separate town in its own right. That's the date to remember: 1851 is the town's incorporation year, and 1651 is when people first put down roots here. (Source: Cromwell Town Profile ↗(opens in a new tab))

Why the river matters

Cromwell's 13.5 square miles sit along the Connecticut River, the defining geographic feature of the town. The river shaped where the early settlement formed, and it still frames how the town looks and where its open space is.

Government

Cromwell operates under a Town Council / Town Manager form of government — an elected Town Council sets policy, and a Town Manager runs the day-to-day administration of the town. Cromwell has no mayor. The change from the former Board of Selectmen took effect with the November 2013 general election. (Source: Form of Government ↗(opens in a new tab))

Population

Cromwell's population is 14,225, as counted in the 2020 U.S. Census.

We use the official 2020 Census figure deliberately. It's the most recent full count published by the U.S. Census Bureau, and it's the number the town's own reporting aligns with. If you see a different figure elsewhere, it's likely from an earlier census or an unofficial estimate.

Sources: U.S. Census Bureau — QuickFacts: Cromwell town, Lower Connecticut River Valley Planning Region ↗(opens in a new tab) · Wikipedia — Cromwell, Connecticut ↗(opens in a new tab)

Getting Here

Cromwell's position between two major cities is one of its quiet advantages:

  • ~14 miles south of Hartford — an easy commute to the capital.
  • ~28 miles north of New Haven — close enough for a day or an evening out.
  • On the Connecticut River, in Middlesex County — midway along the state's river valley corridor.
